How the website works, in pictures

Telugu Cinema Updates, Explained

Think of the site as a small newsroom. Reporters fetch facts from outside sources, a fact-checker compares them with Wikipedia, the editor (the admin) can correct or hide anything, and only then is the page laid out and handed to you.

The big picture

Every page you see is assembled fresh from three places: outside websites that know about films, the site's own notebook where the admin's decisions and saved results live, and a set of rules that decide what is trustworthy enough to show.

What happens when you open the homepage

Here is the full journey, step by step, from the moment you tap the link to the moment the page appears. The numbers are the real order the code runs in.

1

Your request arrives at the door

A guard checks one thing before anything else: are you heading somewhere private? The homepage and movie pages are open to everyone. Only the Account page and the Admin console need you to be signed in.

2

Reporters go and gather the raw lists

The site asks TMDB, a large public movie database, for Telugu-language films released in India. It asks four times, once for each shelf on the homepage: Latest releases, Popular, Upcoming and Top rated. It flips through several pages of results until it has enough, then removes duplicates.

TMDB is crowd-edited, so this raw list contains mistakes: films marked Telugu that are not, half-finished placeholder entries, and dates that later changed. That is why the next steps exist.

3

The fact-checker compares Latest releases with Wikipedia

For released films, a title only counts as real if it appears on Wikipedia's page "List of Telugu films of 2026". The site reads that page's tables, then compares every TMDB title with every Wikipedia title and scores how alike they are.

4

Upcoming films get a different test

A film that has not released yet cannot be on a "films of the year" list, so the site uses two release calendars instead and grades each candidate into one of four bins, in this order.

5

The editor's notebook is applied

Before anything is sorted, every list passes through the admin's saved decisions. This happens outside the long-lived caches, so an admin change shows up on the very next visit.

6

Missing pictures are filled in

Every film card needs a poster and a wide banner image. When TMDB has both, they are used. When either is missing, the site searches Google Images for the film's title and year. When even that finds nothing, a neutral grey placeholder is shown rather than a broken image.

7

The Trending shelf is ordered in three bands

"Trending" is not a guess. It is built from three signals stacked in a fixed order: what just released, what people are talking about, and what the admin has pinned.

8

Finishing touches from other sources

With the lists settled, the site adds the extras the homepage shows around them:

Star ratings

  • Each visible film is looked up on IMDb through the OMDb service to get its IMDb score.
  • Only the films on screen are looked up, not the whole catalogue.

The big banner at the top

  • Featured films get their cast, best banner image and a trailer.
  • Trailers are found on YouTube, preferring official studio channels. The admin can swap the banner or the trailer.

Latest stories

  • Headlines are read from three Telugu news sites: Gulte, 123telugu and TeluguOne.
  • Duplicates are removed and the newest stories come first.
9

The page is laid out and sent to you

Only now is the visual page built: the hero banner, four movie shelves, the stories feed and the sidebar widgets. Your browser receives the finished result and shows it.

Most of this work is remembered for a while so the next visitor does not wait. The checked catalogue is kept for 6 hours, Wikipedia pages for 6 hours and the AndhraBoxOffice calendar for a day. The admin's edits and hidden films are never cached, so they take effect immediately.

TMDBSite's own rule

All of these lookups run at the same time rather than one after another, which is why the page still loads quickly despite asking so many places. If any single source fails, that section is simply left empty and the rest of the page still appears.

The work that happens while nobody is looking

Every three hours, an alarm clock wakes up a background job. It does two slow tasks that would make pages too sluggish if they ran while you waited.

The job is protected by a secret key, so only the scheduler can trigger it. When it finishes verifying a new quarter, it clears the saved catalogue so the next visitor sees the newly confirmed films.

Accounts, and what the admin can do

The site has two kinds of people. Anyone can create an account. A small number of accounts are marked as admins, and only they see the editing tools.

Anyone

  • Register with a name, email and password. Passwords are stored scrambled, never as typed.
  • Log in and stay logged in through a cookie that expires after a while.
  • Forgot password: an email carries a one-time reset link.
  • An Account page to update their own profile.
  • Repeated failed logins from one place are slowed down to stop guessing.

Admins only

  • Movies: add a film TMDB lacks, hide a wrong one, correct its release date, add alternate titles, edit its facts and company credits.
  • Pictures: choose which TMDB banner to use, or upload their own poster or banner.
  • Videos: search YouTube, attach a trailer or song, or hide an unwanted video.
  • Trending: pin unreleased films to fixed slots.
  • Users: view, search, create, promote, disable or delete accounts.
Where the notebook lives: on a developer's own computer it is a single file (SQLite). When the site is deployed online it uses Supabase, a hosted database. The code is the same either way; only the address changes.
